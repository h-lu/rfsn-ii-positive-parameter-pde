# Finite-boundary canard coincidence by multiple shooting

## Frozen object

At

\[
  \epsilon=1,\qquad r=0.08,\qquad q_0=-80,
\]

fix the Appendix-A.3 outer boundary

\[
 u_*=16.645083364843376,\qquad
 v_*=u_*^2+\frac{r^2}{3}u_*^3,
\]

and choose the unique negative value of (p_*) for which (H_2=0).
The unknowns are (a_2), the total flight time, and the four state
coordinates at 79 interior nodes.  The retained equations are the four
continuity defects generated by the **same exact finite-(r) central ODE**
on each of 80 segments, followed by

\[
 p_2(T)=q_2(T)=0.
\]

The earlier collocation BVP supplies only the initial node values.  Its
differential residual is not accepted as evidence for the result below.
The section, unknowns, scales, tolerances, derivative steps, and acceptance
thresholds were frozen in commit `7cb2543` before the retained run.

## Retained result

The 80-segment coincidence equations pass every frozen root and tight-replay
gate at

\[
 a_2=-0.00833819526697979,qquad
 T=14.290555625888304.
\]

The largest unscaled continuity defect is
(1.0851\times10^{-11}), and an independent per-segment replay with tighter
IVP tolerances gives (1.0885\times10^{-11}).  At the endpoint,

\[
 (p_2,q_2)=
 (-3.2882\times10^{-13},-2.1004\times10^{-15}).
\]

The maximum absolute Hamiltonian value at the shooting nodes is
(4.1924\times10^{-10}).  Thus the old A.3 collocation curve does contain a
high-quality **finite-boundary, floating-point multiple-shoot coincidence
candidate**.  This is stronger than accepting the old global collocation
interpolant or an unstable one-shot IVP replay.

## Strict numerical stop at the simple-zero test

For the local splitting family, keep (a_2) fixed, retain the same 80 exact
IVP matching equations, impose only (p_2(T)=0), and define

\[
 S(a_2)=q_2(T).
\]

The linearized fixed-parameter multiple-shoot Jacobian has estimated
2-condition number

\[
 3.8931\times10^{16}.
\]

Implicit differentiation gives
(-3.5383\times10^{13}), whereas the frozen symmetric differences give
(8.2847\times10^3) for (h=10^{-6}) and (8.2819\times10^3) for
(h=5\times10^{-7}).  Moreover, the perturbed fixed-parameter solves stop
with scaled residuals between (7.81\times10^{-8}) and
(1.56\times10^{-7}), above the predeclared (10^{-8}) threshold.
Consequently the derivative check fails closed:

```text
COMPUTED: finite-boundary multiple-shoot coincidence candidate
INCONCLUSIVE: simple zero of the splitting
NOT ESTABLISHED: intrinsic W^cu = W^cs maximal canard
```

The derivative failure is numerical ill-conditioning, not evidence that a
finite-(r) maximal canard is absent.

## Relation to the Vo--Doelman--Kaper result

The published persistence lemma states the asymptotic coincidence of the
relevant center-unstable and center-stable manifolds at

\[
 a_c(\delta)=1-\frac{5\epsilon}{48}\delta^2+\mathcal O(\delta^3),
\]

and the accompanying slow-manifold calculation identifies the same leading
coefficient by pole cancellation.  In the repository's frozen central
scaling this predicts (a_{2,c}^{\rm lead}=-5r/48=-1/120), only
(4.862\times10^{-6}) from the multiple-shoot value above.  The computation
therefore gives a finite-(r) numerical check of the predicted location.
It does not import the theorem's intrinsic branch identification: the
artificial boundary (q_0=-80) still has to be replaced by, or rigorously
connected to, the selected (W^{cu}) trace.

No interval shadowing theorem, connection to the preregistered high-winding
orbit, or PDE pattern-selection conclusion is claimed.
