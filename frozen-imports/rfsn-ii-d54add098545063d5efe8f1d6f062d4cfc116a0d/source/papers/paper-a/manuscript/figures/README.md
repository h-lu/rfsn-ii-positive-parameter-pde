# Paper A figures

The six PDF figures are reproducible vector graphics generated by
`make_figures.py`.  Wall-clock PDF metadata is suppressed so repeated runs
with the same Python, NumPy, Matplotlib, and font stack are byte-for-byte
stable.  Five figures are explanatory diagrams.  The sixth reads stored
interval enclosures from the repository's machine-readable certificate
records; it displays those records but is not itself part of the
computer-assisted proof.  Their claim status is determined by the
fail-closed replay profile documented in the manuscript, not by the plot.

From this directory, run

```bash
python3 make_figures.py
```

The script requires Python 3, NumPy, and Matplotlib.  The checked-in PDFs
were generated with Matplotlib 3.11.1.  The generator asserts a minimum
source label size of 7.2 pt.  Text is embedded and subset, and color
distinctions are repeated with line styles, hatching, markers, or direct
labels so that the figures remain legible in grayscale.

Semantic status:

- `recurrence_exit_geometry.pdf`: schematic incidence diagram;
- `signed_seam_crossform.pdf`: leading linear profiles plus schematic
  source- and target-coordinate geometry at the zero-action boundary;
- `two_end_action_finite_parts.pdf`: schematic comparison of the two
  end-specific finite-part constructions and their compatibility with
  exact action composition;
- `proof_dependency.pdf`: schematic route from intrinsic physical data
  through local exact symplectic coordinates and the return--exit analysis
  to compatible local descriptions of the physical long-passage relation;
  the selected reversible branch remains separate;
- `spiral_fold_mechanism.pdf`: leading-order asymptotic mechanism, plotted
  for the universal core `alpha=beta` while displaying the general slope
  and successive-radius formula for the critical points of the restricted
  Hamiltonian;
- `validated_source_phase_separation.pdf`: stored interval certificate data
  in the common linearizing phase gauge; replay status is stated in the
  manuscript.

Each manuscript caption states the corresponding status and the
interpretive limits of the drawing.

## Mathematical encoding

### Zero-action boundary and cross form

Panel (b) uses source coordinates `(phi,nu)`.  It displays the branchwise
transverse-action scale `g_a=epsilon_a gtilde_a`.  Panel (c) instead uses
target coordinates `(x',bar-nu)`: the horizontal zero-action boundary
`bar-nu=0` cuts the closure of the return strip in the cut-open target
section into its positive- and negative-target-sign components.  No source
cross-form equation is drawn in panel (c).  All strip shapes are schematic.

### Finite parts at the two ends

Panel (a) of `two_end_action_finite_parts.pdf` compares an actual algebraic
tail and its reference tail at the same cutoff `e=epsilon`, starting from a
fixed cut `e=e_*`.  The two curves are distinct; the difference of their
action integrals supplies the algebraic finite part.  Panel (b) uses physical
remaining time `s=t_b-t` at the pole and subtracts the end-specific
Laurent--log counterterm.  Panel (c) applies exact action composition at
every finite cutoff and only then subtracts the terminal end counterterm
before taking the limit.  The algebraic and pole counterterms are different
objects; no equality between them is encoded.  In both cases the schematic
identity is `B_ren = lim_(epsilon -> 0) (B_epsilon - C_B,epsilon)`.

### Selected reversible branch and restricted-Hamiltonian extrema

The plotted curves use the universal-core relation `alpha=beta`.  Open
circles mark maxima and open squares mark minima of the leading restricted
Hamiltonian.  The marker distinction, not color, carries the alternating
meaning.  The general relations are

```text
theta ~ (beta/alpha) log(r) + theta_*,
r_(m+1)/r_m ~ exp(-pi alpha/(2 beta)).
```

The figure is asymptotic and is not a rigorous enclosure of the finite
continuation of the selected branch.

### Stored source-phase certificate data

`validated_source_phase_separation.pdf` reads, and cross-checks where the
same interval is recorded twice, the following files:

```text
validation/origin-algebraic-heteroclinic/certificate.json
validation/universal-core-symmetric-homoclinic/certificate.json
validation/origin-unstable-pole-entry/certificate.json
```

All three source sets lie on `||u||=0.01` in the same linearizing phase
gauge.  The overview subtracts `2*pi` from the algebraic and homoclinic root
boxes and leaves the pole-entry cover on `[-0.2,0.2]`.  Panels (b) and (c)
magnify the full root enclosures around their interval midpoints.  A black
tick is only the arithmetic midpoint used to locate an interval; it is not
a separately enclosed orbit or a floating-point shooting claim.  The
script aborts if the repeated algebraic or homoclinic phase enclosures
disagree between the machine-readable records.

## Caption drafts

`recurrence_exit_geometry.pdf`: **Fixed local return--exit geometry.** A
point in the punctured incoming section makes a long saddle-focus passage
and then either returns through the homoclinic tube or first reaches the
algebraic sheet, pole window, a lateral face, or the target-sign/stable-
manifold cut.  Return edges are denoted directly by their source sign,
winding, and target sign; terminal labels are `alg.`, `pole`, `out`, `rbox`,
and `cut`.  The terminals are not return edges; the drawing is a fixed-flow-
tube schematic, not a global phase-space partition.

`signed_seam_crossform.pdf`: **Opposite endpoint data and the
transverse-action scale.**  (a) Leading stable and unstable profiles for
the variation-of-constants equations with data imposed at opposite
endpoints; they are not nonlinear trajectories.  (b) Schematic return
strips in source coordinates accumulate on the directly labelled positive
and negative sides `nu>0` and `nu<0` at the zero-action boundary `nu=0`, on
the natural exponential scale `g_a=epsilon_a gtilde_a`, with
`epsilon_a asymp exp(-2*pi*alpha*n/beta)`.  (c) In target coordinates the
horizontal zero-action boundary `bar-nu=0` cuts the closure of one return
strip in the cut-open section into its two target-sign components; their
common boundary is the first-exit set `I_(sigma,n)`.  Strip shapes and
distances are schematic.

`two_end_action_finite_parts.pdf`: **Finite parts at the two singular
ends.**  In both cases
`B_ren = lim_(epsilon -> 0) (B_epsilon - C_B,epsilon)`, with an
end-specific counterterm.  (a) At the algebraic end, distinct actual and reference tails are
compared at the same `e=epsilon` after starting from the fixed cut `e=e_*`.
(b) At the pole, the physical remaining time `s=t_b-t` is used and the
end-specific Laurent--log counterterm is subtracted.  (c) Exact action
composition holds at each finite cutoff; the appropriate terminal
counterterm is then subtracted before the cutoff tends to the end.  The two
counterterms are end-specific and are not asserted to be equal.  All three
panels are schematic.

`spiral_fold_mechanism.pdf`: **Restricted-Hamiltonian critical points on
the logarithmic spiral.**
The curves are plotted for the universal core `alpha=beta`; the displayed
formulas give the general slope and radius ratio.  Open circles and squares
denote, respectively, maxima and minima of the leading restriction of `H`.
They are critical points of the scalar restriction, not zeros of `H`.  This
asymptotic diagram is not an interval enclosure of the finite continuation.

`proof_dependency.pdf`: **Route through the theorem.** Solid arrows record
the logical direction from intrinsic physical data to a finite
parameter-local family of exact symplectic coordinates, the
return--exit/time/action analysis, compatible coordinate changes, and the
physical long-passage relation.  The
dashed arrow records verification of the intrinsic hypotheses for the
normal-form system.  The diagram does not derive either end from the
saddle-focus germ and does not posit a global winding label.  The Jost
analysis and continuation of the selected reversible branch are shown in a
separate box because they are not used in the return--exit theorem.

`validated_source_phase_separation.pdf`: **Stored source-phase certificate
data.** On the circle `||u||=0.01`, panel (a) places the interval-enclosed
algebraic connection, symmetric homoclinic, and closed pole-entry window in
their common phase gauge; the first two are shown after subtracting `2*pi`.
Arrows report the lower bounds stored in the certificate records.  Panels
(b) and (c) magnify the complete algebraic and homoclinic enclosures around
their midpoints.  Thick segments are interval enclosures; black ticks are
midpoints used only for plotting.  The data are read from the three
machine-readable records named above; the manuscript reports the
fail-closed replay status.
